# GODBOX settlement streets and SVG inspection

This pass improves the production relationship between circulation, plots and building fronts. It also fixes SVG inspection in the architecture browser and extends coherent composition to classical through industrial workshops.

## Browser controls

Open [the architecture browser](http://127.0.0.1:5175/architecture-browser.html?view=street&archetype=house&period=medieval&material-scenario=timber&culture=river&climate=temperate&seed=street-review).

- **Street** shows a mixed neighbourhood on real simulation-reserved plots. The viewer declares a movement-history snapshot; it uses production plot reservation, building fit, facade composition, street geometry and shaders. It does not claim that the snapshot was generated by a running economy.
- **Solo** isolates the selected building for architectural inspection. Gallery, catalogue, timeline and material comparisons remain available.
- **SVG facade detailing** removes SVG material bindings when unchecked and restores them when checked. Geometry, openings, construction pieces and LODs remain intact. This also works after changing buildings or rebuilding while SVG is off.
- **Neutral materials / inspect geometry** replaces building surfaces with neutral materials. Glass remains faintly translucent so physical openings are readable.
- A coverage readout reports how many visible buildings actually have SVG, pending rasterization, failures and budget fallbacks. The inspector explicitly identifies buildings without SVG. Turning SVG on does not invent detailing on ineligible structures.

The old four-atlas owner budget could be exhausted by earlier previews, silently leaving subsequent eligible buildings without SVG. Each browser rebuild now releases the previous asset owner and starts a bounded review cache (64 entries, 512-pixel rasters). Production simulation defaults remain four entries at 1024 pixels. Disabled previews use their original production shaders. Newly created clones retain the information needed to restore their SVG materials.

Recessed glass is projected at its actual physical depth. Wall artwork is restricted to wall and structural-frame assembly pieces; counters, crates, shutters, foundations and roof joinery retain their original surfaces. Facade artwork is masked off on back-facing surfaces, preventing exterior artwork from appearing on interior faces seen through a doorway.

## Production street changes

The shared movement survey retains the strongest recorded links, then minimally joins neighbouring worn components. This fixes broken lanes on equal-wear plateaus without stamping a grid, crossing unmarked cells or granting paving. Renderer geometry and plot frontage use this same survey. Water and flooded cells are excluded.

Lanes share bounded cross-sections at bends. Small polygon fans fill branch junctions; acute miters cannot produce long spikes. All surface vertices sample terrain. Shared longitudinal stations keep ordinary paving courses continuous through bends. Street type, width and surface treatment still come from accumulated wear and paid road development, not the viewer's historical period or a semantic district label. The road surface remains one batched mesh.

New reservations store the accessible frontage point and entrance orientation at founding. Upgrades preserve these. The renderer gives older saves a first-presentation facing from nearby circulation, then retains its cached orientation. Isolated compounds retain their local-anchor fallback. No road is created by reserving or orienting a building.

## Workshop architecture

Classical through industrial enclosed workshops now resolve a **work-hall** program. Working entrances, rear service access and bench-height daylight cuts replace generic facade grids. Early guild halls have a single taller working storey; later workshops can stack working floors. Roofs, frames, floor decks, foundations and equipment use the existing physical emitter and canonical construction stages. Counters sit beside the entrance, and crates stay clear of its passage.

This removes the legacy pile of facade braces, repeated bands, roof ornaments, duplicate flues and arbitrary annex massing from these workshop cores. Open early sheds and later industrial roof types retain their existing paths. Available materials and historical technology still resolve in BuildingSpec; SVG is optional surface artwork.

## Validation

The broader selected suite passes **257 tests across 18 files**. Checks cover a connected wear plateau, shared renderer/simulation frontage, dry circulation authority, corner and junction coverage, bounded acute joins, terrain conformity, longitudinal continuity, deterministic stored entrance orientation, actual working-hall cuts and clear entrance access, and material detach/restore on clones created while disabled. Existing architecture, construction, sleeping, LOD, render-budget and material-authority tests are included. The closing facade/browser/street suite also passes all 24 tests, including protection against repainting nearby fitted equipment.

The final browser pass captures **18 views** and verifies material removal and restoration, a visible SVG difference where SVG exists, unchanged draw calls/triangle counts, and rebuilding while disabled. [Machine-readable browser report](webgl-report.json). The two retained before images are additional evidence, not part of that final count. Build and lint pass; the build retains existing bundle-size and mixed-import warnings. This is selected regression validation, not a claim that the whole simulation suite is green.

## Limits

This establishes a stronger street and inspection pipeline; the whole catalogue is not uniformly world class. Circulation remains a reconstruction from coarse cell wear, with minimal inferred adjacency rather than saved individual footstep curves. Old plots preserve their locations. Deliberate sidewalks, drainage works, dense attached urban blocks and civic street projects still need construction authority and further production work. Regional craft variants and modern industrial workshop forms need the same architectural scrutiny as these new working halls. The flat review ground isolates the buildings and circulation; a naturally evolved terrain settlement remains the broader visual acceptance test.
