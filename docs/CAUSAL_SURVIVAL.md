# Consequence-driven survival foundation

## Audit before implementation

The monthly simulation in `src/sim/Simulation.ts` already connects many systems. The useful unit of intervention is its existing settlement economy and shared labour budget, not a replacement civilization controller. The following audit describes the starting implementation. “Gap” means an absent or weak causal connection, not that the entire system needs replacing.

| System | Demand and consequences of ignoring it | Perception, decisions and alternatives | Feedback and historical evidence / gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| People and agents | Health, age, households, work, travel and displacement affect survival. | People choose destinations; severe weather interrupts travel; identity follows occupation and capabilities. | Health reduces effective labour. Personal memories capture relationships and witnessed events. Food energy was weakly connected to actual missed consumption. |
| Settlements and population | Local food security, prosperity, capacity, conflict and climate affect births, migration and abandonment. | Settlement economy and annual politics read these signals. | Growth creates demand and strain. Explicit and statistical population have separate authorities; display representatives must never count as extra citizens. |
| Inventory / processing / consumption | Canonical typed inventory, finite deposits, recipes, fuel and shared labour constrain productive work. Medicines improve health; shortages hurt prosperity. | Demand queues, known deposits, access routes and recipe requirements select feasible work. | Extraction depletes deposits and damages ecology; failed experiments consume inputs. Resource events carry input and location evidence. Food is a separate bulk resource, not typed material inventory. |
| Food and agriculture | Farmers/foragers produce; population consumes; weather, soil water and knowledge change output. Reserves affect fertility, health and migration. | `foodSecurity`, balances and crisis months feed several systems. | Food was clamped at zero before unmet consumption could be measured. Water revises production later, after the original clamp. Generic buildings provided storage capacity, so a food store lacked a distinct food-preservation benefit. |
| Gathering and resource scarcity | Discovery, geography, extraction knowledge, labour, depletion and transport determine material delivery. | Alternative known deposits and imports exist; exhaustion creates migration and territorial incentives. | Work assignments support visible extraction. Existing food foraging remains aggregate; it does not deplete an explicit wild-food deposit. |
| Construction / settlement development | Population, institutions, livelihoods and unmet services select projects. Material, labour, sponsor and placement checks can block them. | Annual local evaluation; multiple informal providers and culturally different building forms. | Actual persistent sites, repairs, ruins and project histories exist. Housing service primarily influenced development, rather than directly mitigating cold mortality. Selection among building needs is largely deterministic. |
| Knowledge / technology | Practice, experimentation, transmission, preservation, prerequisites and adoption stages gate capabilities. Pressure already affects some research domains. | Specialists, institutions, local capabilities and contact matter; inventions may fail or disappear. | Medicine, production and transport feed back into society. Some experimentation grows from generic curiosity or pressure without measuring a particular attempted remedy. Do not replace the working adoption model with a new unlock tree. |
| Professions and roles | Shared labour accounting and acquired expertise make workers productive; retirement and adulthood change careers. | Ordinary adults keep careers, with return-to-work transitions after inactivity. Render roles are richer than economic occupation buckets. | Stable careers and apprenticeship are valuable, but urgent shortages rarely divert work or recruit new specialists. A healer role is not a complete disease-treatment system. |
| Health, injuries and death | Health changes with nutrition, water, medicine, pollution, displacement and disasters; war kills through the lifecycle authority. | Medical knowledge and remedy production offer mitigation. | Health affects labour and fertility. No general pathogen transmission, injury/disability progression or childbirth-injury model was found. Modern pandemics are aggregate risk events. |
| Weather, seasons, temperature | Seasonal production, snow, flooding, storms and tornadoes affect work, travel, crops and structures. | Agents shelter from severe weather; settlements repair damage. | Weather has real consequences and events. Normalized temperature existed, but sustained cold lacked a household shelter/fuel/health loop. |
| Water and environment | Hydrology changes crop output, water quality, sanitation, health and drought. Logging, mining and land use damage the environment. | Water buildings and knowledge mitigate several problems. | Existing environmental feedback is substantial. Food production adjustment needed integration with a consumption ledger; it must not silently consume or create missing food. |
| Trade | Uneven stocks, specialization, relations and connected transport enable exchange. Actual delivery gates diffusion. | Exchanges, alternate partners, migration and local production are existing responses. | Trade creates dependence, merchant influence, cultural contact and knowledge transfer. Food risk need not create a magical route or unearned imports. |
| Migration | Scarcity, density, danger, family ties and opportunity affect departure. | Destination appeal and passability constrain movement; staying remains possible. | Migrants carry culture and knowledge; abandonment and displacement have consequences. Existing reasons are recorded, but not every migration points to a specific prior pressure event. |
| Social organization | Kinship, relationships, influence, population and cooperation support coordination. | Councils, informal organization and other institutions compete indirectly. | Social ties support learning and collective action. No general property-dispute or collective-action accounting exists. |
| Warfare and diplomacy | Scarcity, territorial tensions, retaliation, ambition and strategic resources drive hostility. | Contact, trust, trade, alliances, campaigns, retreat and peace exist. | Reserved labour, supply, deaths, destruction, displacement, tribute and exhaustion are real costs. Campaign memory is unusually strong existing causal infrastructure. Do not duplicate it with a generic threat controller. |
| Religion / beliefs | Religious tendency, temple support, rituals, shared identity and personal values influence behavior. | Informal rituals, temples and religious forms of care/order exist. | Cultural tendencies change with experience. Many beliefs remain dispositions rather than explanations tested against a lived problem. |
| Government / institutions | Coordination load, trade, specialized production, external hostility and cultural preferences promote institutions. | Existing councils, temples, merchant associations, military orders, craft circles and knowledge keepers. | Institutions consume resources, shape power and sponsor buildings. Several formation scores still rely heavily on population or personality; constitutions and administrations lack detailed operating costs. |
| Law / crime | Security needs and guard/police building names exist. | No general theft, property, adjudication or enforcement loop was found. | Adding police before a real crime/dispute system would reproduce the consequence gap. Deferred. |
| Historian / persistent history | Historical events include causes, context, actors and outcomes. Historian validates source events/entities. | Significant event types become scenes. RunArchive observes emissions before hot-history pruning. | Archive stores settlement/culture snapshots and important events. Some old food summaries asserted rationing without an actual policy; corrected here. Causal parent IDs can use existing causes/context fields; no second history database is needed. |

## Implemented scope and order

1. At the monthly boundary, settlements perceive local food reserves, previous net flow and accumulated deprivation. `PressureObservation` records actual/perceived intensity, confidence, duration, trend, urgency, expected severity, population, location, evidence, consequences and known response categories. Food expectation is a simple extrapolation, not an oracle for future weather. Cold uses current local weather and functioning shelter.
2. A settlement may choose foraging, cultivation, rationing or delay. Choices use an isolated seed derived from run seed, settlement and month; local fertility, land, workers, adopted practical knowledge, urgency, cultural preferences and learned outcomes affect feasibility/weights. Active responses last three months, with a six-month decision interval. Wait remains possible under severe need. Existing trade, migration and diplomacy continue independently.
3. Food work diverts a bounded fraction of builder/artisan/carrier/keeper **economy** time at 70% efficiency for unfamiliar work. It cannot spend resource extraction, military, infrastructure or industrial reservations twice. Reduced crafts, transport support and building work are immediate opportunity costs. Temporary collective effort does not grant personal expertise or change occupation labels.
4. The food ledger measures full need, planned ration, production, spoilage and actual intake before clamping reserves. Rationing reduces planned consumption by 12%; those missed calories still count as deprivation. Functioning food stores add capacity and reduce spoilage. Water revises production through the same ledger. Trade imports can satisfy this month's remaining planned intake before consequences settle.
5. Deprivation accumulates fractional months of missed nutrition, capped at twelve. Fully fed months repay 0.35 months of debt. Work effectiveness falls through the existing health contract; fertility falls and additional mortality starts after sustained deprivation. Cold consumes real timber only with practical fire knowledge, and functioning housing/fuel independently reduce exposure. Cold affects exposed production, health and eventually mortality. Nothing guarantees rescue.
6. Response resolution records measured marginal food contribution, food retained by rationing, effort diverted and before/after observed risk. A positive contribution plus lower risk reinforces the response; failure reduces its preference. The event explicitly distinguishes directly measured contribution from overall improvement, which can also reflect weather, trade and population changes.
7. Sustained undernutrition creates a source-linked memory in the existing Culture memory object. It fades slowly, raises later food-infrastructure priority and probabilistically favors storage over fields. Repeated successful food responses can recruit an existing adult into a food occupation, using existing career/identity/expertise systems. Skill must subsequently be learned normally. This pass does not invent courts, taxation or medical institutions from a shortage counter.
8. Four new event types use the existing event/observer/archive path: pressure detected, response attempted, response resolved, adaptation established. Attempts link to pressure and learned-outcome events; outcomes link to their attempts; specialization links to demonstrated response history. Construction reasons can link to remembered famine. Narration only claims recorded actions and measurements.

## Boundaries and compatibility

- Optional `Settlement.survival` and `Culture.memory.foodScarcity` fields default lazily. Old snapshots remain readable; no archive schema replacement is required. New simulation rules require a new engine version, so newly generated histories are not advertised as replays of old engine histories.
- This is a settlement-level intake model with equal nutritional exposure, not individual caloric inventories, preferential ration distribution or an inequality model. Existing health differences still produce different outcomes.
- Structural services and cold state are presentation hooks. Cold also uses the existing shelter behavior. Temporary collective food work is visible in settlement labour telemetry; individual animations do not yet depict each transferred worker.
- Food production remains the existing aggregate farming/foraging model. Explicit wild-food depletion, crop area, preservation recipes, disease transmission, injury histories, crime, policing and new institutions are deferred. Existing warfare/diplomacy continue to own their pressures and consequences.
- The current month consumes food before the end-of-month trade stage. Delivered imports can refill missed intake; an export cannot take food already consumed. Water modifies harvest yield rather than applying irrigation to consumption.
- Statistical citizens receive deprivation mortality through the existing aggregate demographic authority, independently of carrying-capacity growth. Documentary agent deaths do not deduct another represented population. Aggregate workforce profiles remain authoritative; this pass recruits individual specialists only before the statistical transition.
- Local response memory has four bounded entries; pressure doses/durations are bounded. No historical scan is used to make a decision. History/archives retain documentary events under existing policies; a parent evicted from hot history remains available in the archive observer's record.

## Validation

`tests/pressures.test.ts` covers warning before hunger, ignored scarcity, gradual mortality/recovery, conservation, late water adjustment and imports, real shelter/fuel/storage effects, feasible divergent choices, conserved labour, learned preferences, specialization, grounded history and archive retention, finite values, bounded local evaluation, deterministic full simulation and actual starvation deaths. See the final validation notes below for broad-suite results and pre-existing failures.

Baseline before edits: the focused resource/human-capital/development/weather/Historian/archive slice had 67 passing tests and 9 failures, all in `settlement-development.test.ts`. Seven failures reported `material processing conservation violation: saw-lumber:timber`; two asserted stale bulk-material balances. These preceded this feature and are investigated separately if they obstruct integration.
